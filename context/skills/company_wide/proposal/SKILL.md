# Write Proposal

Turn the user's notes into a practical proposal that the intended reader can evaluate and act on. Default to a short Markdown document, roughly one page, unless the user requests another format or length.

## Approach

- Use the supplied context to identify the audience, problem, desired outcome, proposed work, and constraints.
- Ask only for missing details that materially change the recommendation. Otherwise, draft with clearly labeled assumptions or concise `[TBD]` placeholders.
- Lead with the recommendation and its expected benefit. Connect each deliverable to the problem it solves.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions and estimates. Do not invent prices, deadlines, metrics, credentials, or commitments.
- Use concrete, plain language. Avoid sales hype, generic introductions, and unsupported promises.

## Default structure

Adapt these sections to the request; combine or omit sections that add no value.

1. **Proposal:** A specific title and a brief statement of the recommended action and intended outcome.
2. **Problem:** What needs to change, who it affects, and why it matters now.
3. **Proposed work:** The approach and tangible deliverables. Include a simple success measure when the context supports one.
4. **Scope:** What is included, important exclusions, and required inputs or dependencies.
5. **Timeline and cost:** Known milestones and budget. Label estimates and state their assumptions; use `[TBD]` for unknown commitments.
6. **Next step:** The specific decision or action needed to proceed, with an owner if known.

## Final pass

Check that the scope supports the promised outcome, timeline and cost agree with the scope, and the reader can tell what they are being asked to approve. Keep unresolved details visible. Return the proposal itself without a preamble unless explanation is requested.

Creating a proposal does not authorize sending it, accepting terms, or beginning the proposed work.
